# Technical Writing

Write for a specific reader and purpose. Keep technical meaning intact while making the argument easy to follow. Human-readable research can remain formal and precise; it does not need conversational filler.

## Choose the guidance that fits

Read the relevant reference before drafting or reviewing. Do not load the whole collection.

| Deliverable | Reference | Main question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Research paper, abstract, thesis section, related work, reviewer response | [Research papers](references/research-papers.md) | What does the evidence support, and what can another researcher reproduce? |

## Establish the brief from available context

Identify the audience, their existing knowledge, the document's purpose, its stage, and any venue, template, length, or voice constraints. Inspect supplied drafts, notes, results, code, figures, and examples first. Preserve established project and venue conventions.

Infer ordinary choices and start useful work. Ask only when missing information changes the argument or prevents a defensible draft; group essential questions. Do not require an interview, outline approval, section-by-section confirmation, or a new workflow for an ordinary writing request. A request to polish one paragraph does not authorize a manuscript-wide rewrite or a fresh literature review.

## Develop the substance before polishing

- Find the claim, question, decision, or teaching goal that holds the document together. Arrange the material around what the reader needs next; do not fill a generic template with empty sections.
- Separate supplied facts, verified source evidence, interpretation, proposals, and unknowns. Never invent results, citations, methods, causes, commitments, or personal experience to make prose sound complete.
- In an edit, preserve numbers, units, denominators, comparisons, conditions, uncertainty, attribution, and decision status. Keep code, commands, identifiers, equations, and link targets unchanged unless the task includes correcting them.
- If source material contradicts itself, expose the conflict. If it contains a false or unsupported claim, flag or qualify it rather than silently endorsing it. Do not turn a language edit into an unrequested redesign or analysis.
- Use stable technical terms. Explain unfamiliar terms at the point of need, at a depth suitable for this audience. Do not rotate synonyms for the same entity or replace precise terminology with a vague everyday word.

## Make the draft readable

State the point early at the level appropriate to the genre. Connect paragraphs so the reader can see how the evidence or reasoning advances it. Favor concrete subjects and verbs, coherent paragraphs, and sentences whose length follows the thought. Use headings, lists, tables, and figures when they help navigation or comparison and the requested format permits them.

Remove stock openings, inflated significance, unsupported praise, redundant conclusions, vague attribution, and forced rhetorical patterns. Keep useful emphasis, necessary repetition, genuine technical distinctions, and the author's voice. Consult [clear prose](references/clear-prose.md) for a focused editing pass; do not enforce word blacklists, sentence-length ceilings, or a universal ban on passive voice.

## Check and deliver

Reconcile the draft with its sources after editing, especially quantities, qualifiers, and repeated claims. Distinguish checks actually completed from checks that need unavailable evidence. A fluent draft is not evidence that a result is true.

Read once from the intended reader's position: can they identify the main point, follow the reasoning, and understand the limitation or next action without the private conversation? Repair omissions rather than adding background they already know.

Return the requested text or edit the requested file. Keep internal outlines, style diagnostics, and alternate drafts internal unless requested. Add a brief separate note only for material missing evidence, contradictions, or changes the user needs to review. For a critique, give concrete issues, their consequences, and useful revisions instead of silently rewriting everything. Do not add citations to skill repositories in the user's document merely because a skill was used.
